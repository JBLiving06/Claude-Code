# Workshop Exercises

These exercises are designed to be completed with the AI Tutor between video modules. Each exercise takes 15-30 minutes and helps you apply what you just learned.

---

## Post-Module 1 Exercise: Framework Application

**Objective:** Identify where you currently fall on the procedural-to-mastery spectrum and set a specific goal.

### Part 1: Self-Assessment (10 minutes)

Think about the last three times you used AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) for academic work. For each instance, answer:

1. What were you trying to accomplish?
2. How many prompts did you use? (1-2 = procedural, 5+ = iterating toward mastery)
3. Did you verify any of the AI's claims with primary sources?
4. Did the AI help you think differently, or just produce output faster?

### Part 2: Pattern Recognition (5 minutes)

Based on your self-assessment, identify:
- Your most common AI use pattern (probably procedural if you're new to the protocols)
- One specific assignment type where you default to procedural use
- What a mastery approach would look like for that assignment type

### Part 3: Goal Setting (5 minutes)

Complete this statement:

> "For my next [assignment type], I will use the [specific protocol] by [specific action]. I'll know it worked if [specific outcome]."

**Example:** "For my next research paper, I will use the rubric engineering protocol by pasting the assignment and rubric into ChatGPT before I start researching. I'll know it worked if I can articulate what my professor values most before I begin."

### Deliverable

Share your completed goal statement with the AI Tutor. The tutor will help you refine it and identify potential obstacles.

## Post-Module 2 Exercise: Protocol Practice (Rubric Engineering)

**Objective:** Apply the rubric engineering protocol to a real assignment you're currently working on.

### What You'll Need

- An actual assignment you have due in the next 2-3 weeks
- The assignment description and rubric (if provided)
- Access to free ChatGPT

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Gather Materials (2 minutes)**

Find your assignment description and any grading rubric. If there's no rubric, use the assignment description alone—professors reveal priorities even in how they word prompts.

**Step 2: Initial Analysis (5 minutes)**

Paste the assignment and rubric into ChatGPT. Use this exact prompt:

```
Here's an assignment I have for my [course name] class:

[Paste assignment description]

[Paste rubric if available]

Analyze this rubric and assignment. Tell me:
1. What does this professor prioritize? What distinguishes an A from a B?
2. What are the three biggest mistakes a student might make?
3. What's NOT explicitly stated but probably expected?
```

**Step 3: Record the Analysis (5 minutes)**

Write down in your own words:
- Top 3 things the professor cares about
- Top 3 mistakes to avoid
- Any surprises or insights from the analysis

**Step 4: Create Your Checklist (5 minutes)**

Ask ChatGPT:

```
Based on your analysis, give me a pre-submission checklist. What should I verify my paper/assignment includes before I turn it in?
```

Save this checklist—you'll use it before you submit.

**Step 5: Reflection (5 minutes)**

Answer these questions:
- What did the rubric analysis reveal that you wouldn't have noticed on your own?
- How will this change your approach to the assignment?
- What's one thing you'll do differently now?

### Deliverable

Share the following with the AI Tutor:
1. Your assignment topic (not the whole description)
2. The top 3 priorities you identified
3. One insight that surprised you

The tutor will help you verify your analysis and prepare for the next steps.

## Post-Module 3 Exercise: Full Workflow Replication

**Objective:** Complete the entire research paper workflow from Module 3 on a real assignment.

### Prerequisites

- A research paper or essay assignment (2+ pages)
- At least one week until the deadline
- Completion of the rubric engineering exercise

### The Full Workflow

This exercise walks you through the complete process shown in Module 3. Plan to spend 2-3 hours across several sessions.

---

### Phase 1: Rubric Engineering (Done in previous exercise)

If you haven't done this, go back to the Post-Module 2 exercise first.

---

### Phase 2: Research Mapping (30 minutes)

**Step 1:** Get your research map

```
I'm researching [your topic] for a [course] class. Give me a research framework:
- What are the major perspectives or schools of thought on this topic?
- What are the key debates or controversies?
- What types of sources should I look for?
- What search terms should I use in Google Scholar and JSTOR?
```

**Step 2:** Actually search

Open Google Scholar or your library database. Search using the terms ChatGPT suggested. Download 3-5 sources that look relevant (focus on peer-reviewed articles if available).

**Step 3:** Record what you found

List your sources with brief notes on what each one seems to argue.

---

### Phase 3: Source Synthesis (30 minutes)

**Step 1:** Paste abstracts into ChatGPT

```
I'm writing about [topic]. Here are the abstracts of sources I've found:

[Paste abstract 1]
[Paste abstract 2]
[Paste abstract 3]

Based on these sources:
1. What do these authors agree on?
2. Where do they disagree?
3. Are there any gaps—perspectives I'm missing?
4. What thesis angle would let me engage with this conversation rather than just summarize?
```

**Step 2:** Develop your thesis

Based on ChatGPT's analysis, draft a working thesis statement. It should:
- Take a position (not just describe)
- Engage with the sources you found
- Be arguable (someone could disagree)

---

### Phase 4: Outline and Draft (60+ minutes)

**Step 1:** Create an outline

Write a basic outline with:
- Introduction with thesis
- 3-4 body sections, each with a main point and supporting evidence
- Conclusion

**Step 2:** Write your draft

Write the paper yourself. Use your sources. Take your time.

**Step 3:** Get feedback

Paste your draft into ChatGPT (same conversation if possible):

```
Here's my draft of the paper we've been discussing. Evaluate it against the rubric priorities we identified:

[Paste draft]

Be specific: Which sections are strong? Which are weak? What's missing?
```

---

### Phase 5: Revision and Polish (30 minutes)

**Step 1:** Address substantive feedback

Revise based on ChatGPT's feedback. Focus on argument and evidence first.

**Step 2:** Surface-level editing

```
Read this draft and identify:
- Unclear sentences
- Grammar issues
- Awkward transitions
- Any other surface-level problems

[Paste revised draft]
```

**Step 3:** Final check against rubric

```
Compare my final draft to the rubric checklist we created. Am I meeting all the criteria?
```

---

### Deliverable

Share the following with the AI Tutor:
1. Your thesis statement
2. One thing you learned from the research synthesis step
3. One piece of feedback from the draft review that improved your paper
4. Your overall reflection: Did the workflow help? What would you do differently?

## Bonus Exercise: Exam Prep Protocol

**Objective:** Apply the exam preparation protocol to an upcoming exam.

### What You'll Need

- An exam at least 1 week away
- Your notes, syllabus, or study guide
- 2+ hours over several study sessions

### The Process

**Session 1: Generate Practice Questions (30 minutes)**

```
Based on this material from my [course name] course:

[Paste syllabus, notes, or key concepts]

Generate 20 practice questions in the style of a college exam:
- 5 factual recall questions
- 5 application questions (use concepts in new situations)
- 5 comparison/analysis questions
- 5 short-answer questions requiring a paragraph

Don't give me the answers yet.
```

Save these questions.

**Session 2: Active Recall Practice (45 minutes)**

- Answer each question from memory (no notes)
- Write out full answers, even for short questions
- This should feel difficult—that's the point

Then:

```
Here are my answers to your practice questions. Grade each one:
- What's correct?
- What's wrong or incomplete?
- What would a better answer include?

[Paste your answers]
```

**Session 3: Conceptual Mapping (30 minutes)**

```
These are the major topics from my course: [list them]

What are the conceptual links between them? What themes run across the whole course?

Generate 3 possible essay prompts that would ask me to synthesize across these topics.
```

Practice outlining answers to each prompt under time pressure (5 minutes per outline).

**Session 4: Feynman Technique (30 minutes)**

Choose your weakest concepts. For each:

```
I'm going to explain [concept]. Tell me where my explanation is unclear, incomplete, or incorrect:

[Your explanation]
```

---

### Deliverable

After your exam, share with the AI Tutor:
1. How well did the practice questions predict actual exam content?
2. Which step was most valuable?
3. What would you do differently next time?

## Tips for Getting the Most from Exercises

1. **Use real assignments.** Hypotheticals don't build habits. Apply protocols to work you actually need to do.

2. **Don't skip the writing.** When exercises ask you to write or record something, do it. The act of articulating helps you internalize.

3. **Ask the AI Tutor for help.** That's what it's there for. If you're stuck, share your work-in-progress and ask for guidance.

4. **Reflect honestly.** The goal isn't to perform mastery—it's to develop it. Be honest about what's working and what isn't.

5. **Iterate.** The first time you use a protocol might feel clunky. It gets smoother with practice.
